---
title: Payments
description: How a Zapi payment moves from a create request through checkout to a final state.
---

A payment is a request to move money from a payer to a merchant, tracked through
its own lifecycle. Zapi creates it, the payer approves it on a hosted surface,
and the provider moves the money.

Payments are push-style: one create call with `confirm` starts the flow. There is
no separate capture or void step. A payment either succeeds, fails, or is
cancelled before it succeeds.

## The lifecycle

```text
requires_confirmation ──confirm──▶ processing ──▶ succeeded
                                      │
                                      ├──▶ failed
                                      └──▶ cancelled
```

`requires_confirmation` exists only when you create a payment with
`confirm: false`. It has not started and has no payer action yet.

`processing` means the payer or the provider is still acting. The payment may
have a `checkout_url` for the payer, and it may take time for the provider to
answer. Do not fulfil an order in this state.

`succeeded`, `failed`, and `cancelled` are final. A failed payment carries a
`failure_code`; a succeeded payment carries none. A cancelled payment was
cancelled before it succeeded, either by you through the API or by the payer on
the checkout page.

## Checkout

The create request never carries payer details. Phone numbers, bank accounts, and
card numbers are collected on Zapi's hosted checkout. That boundary keeps payer
data out of your servers.

When the payer must act, the payment response includes `checkout_url`. Send the
payer there. When no payer action is required, `checkout_url` is `null`.

If the payer does not act before `expires_at`, the checkout stops accepting them
and the payment ends `failed` with `timed_out`.

## Fulfilment

Fulfil on `payment.success`, not when the create request returns. The create
response arrives while the payment is still `processing`.

Events are delivered at least once. Use the `evt_...` identifier to deduplicate,
and retrieve the payment when you need the current state. See
[Webhooks and events](/docs/concepts/webhooks).

## Refunds

A refund returns money to a payer after a payment succeeds. Refunds are partial,
repeatable, and capped at the captured amount. A refund is its own object
(`refnd_...`) with its own lifecycle, and a payment can have more than one.

## Modes

Every payment carries `livemode`. A payment created with a test key has
`livemode: false` and is isolated from live data. See
[Test mode](/docs/guides/test-mode).

## What the contract does not include

- No capture or void step. Create with `confirm` and cancel before success.
- No payer phone number, bank account, or card field in the request.
- No fee or settlement-time field. Those figures are not published until a
  verified source exists.
